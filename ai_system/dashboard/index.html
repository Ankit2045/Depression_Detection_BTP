🧠 Clinical Depression Subtypes

  • Melancholic Depression: Pervasive psychomotor retardation, profound lack of daytime vigor, prolonged immobility bouts, and early-morning awakening.
  • Agitated / Bipolar Mixed: High motor restlessness, physical tension, nocturnal insomnia, and erratic movement spikes above baseline.
  • Atypical Depression: Delayed sleep-wake schedule, severe morning sleep inertia (08:00–11:00), afternoon crashes, and leaden fatigue.
  • Euthymia: Normal, healthy balance of circadian motor rhythms and restorative sleep.

⚠️ Recognizing Warning Signs

  • Persistent low mood, sadness, emptiness, or pervasive loss of interest (anhedonia).
  • Significant change in movement: noticeable slowing down or restless pacing.
  • Severe sleep disruption: inability to fall asleep, or waking at 03:00 AM unable to return to sleep.
  • Feelings of worthlessness, intense cognitive fog, or thoughts that life is not worth living.
  • Withdrawal from family, studies, work, and essential daily activities.

🛡️ Evidence-Based Measures & Interventions

  • Morning Light Exposure: 30–45 minutes of morning sunlight or 10,000 lux phototherapy at 07:30 AM to anchor the master biological clock.
  • Behavioral Activation: Scheduled daily 20-minute brisk walks at 09:00 AM and 14:30 PM to interrupt prolonged sedentary bouts.
  • Strict Sleep Window (CBT-I): Maintain consistent bedtime and rise times even on weekends; avoid screens 2 hours before bed.
  • Professional Consultation: Schedule an evaluation with a psychiatrist or clinical psychologist for structured MADRS/HAM-D assessment and treatment planning.
